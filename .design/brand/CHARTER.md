# Sans calque: charter

The world is a light table (refs/NOTES.md). A cool frosted French sheet lies over the warm English original.
Anything flagged is the original showing through.

1. **The canvas is the walnut desk in dark mode, and the lit table in light mode.** Never pure black and never pure white,
   because neither appears anywhere in the photos.
2. **Sheets are vellum (#d9e1e4) with a fibre grain.** The French always sits on a sheet. The English sits
   on the warm lit table, never on vellum, because the original is what lies under the tracing paper.
3. **Warm means the original showing through.** Amber (#d7b77d) is used only behind a flagged
   term, where English or a calque bleeds through the French. It is never a button and never decoration.
4. **Vermilion (#c8372f) is the correction pencil.** It draws rings, ticks and underlines on flagged terms
   and nothing else. It is never body text. The darker #a8281f is the only vermilion allowed as text.
5. **The show-through is the signature device.** A flagged term gets an amber patch with a vermilion pencil
   baseline, the original showing through the vellum, and the same mark is used nowhere else. The peg-bar punch survives only in
   the wordmark's q. Punched holes on the sheets were removed on 2026-10-16, after three outside reviews in four rounds read them as
   carousel dots.
6. **Lettering is Osifont** (ISO 3098, the lettering standard on technical drawings), used for the wordmark, labels,
   numbers and buttons. **The French and English text is Spectral**, because it is what a person reads.
7. **Titles carry no full stop and no comma.** Lettering on a drawing is not a sentence.
8. **At most two tracked-caps labels per screen.** ISO lettering is set in plain caps or lower case without
   letter-spacing gimmicks.
9. **Numbers are Osifont with tabular figures**, and always shown with their denominator ("10 of 10", "of 242"),
   because every number here is graded against something.
10. **Depth is physical.** Sheets cast one soft shadow onto the table, and a sheet laid over another sheet is offset by a
    few pixels like a misregistered tracing. No glow, no floating cards, no glass blur.
11. **Illustrations are pencil on vellum, one hand** (art-direction.md). They sit directly on the canvas or on a sheet,
    never on a disc. No code-drawn figures.
12. **One orchestrated moment per view.** It is the French sheet settling down onto the pegs when a translation lands. Nothing
    moves on exit. Reduced motion means the sheet simply appears.
13. **Every flag cites its source** in the same line: Termium Plus, or "N of N government pairs".
    A flag without a source is not shown.
14. **Never on this product:** maple leaves, fleur-de-lis, flags, the colour blue as an accent, lightbulbs,
    globes, or a speech-bubble "translate" icon. These are the clichés of the category and of the country.
